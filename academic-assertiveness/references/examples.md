# Calibration examples

**Defensive scope.** “This study does not attempt to explain neural mechanisms. We examine associations between attention and performance.” → “We examine associations between attention and performance.” Retain “The design does not identify neural mechanisms” if readers might otherwise infer a mechanistic finding.

**Redundant hedge.** “The findings may possibly reflect sampling variation.” → “The findings may reflect sampling variation.” Do not replace “may reflect” with “reflect.”

**Necessary uncertainty.** “The association may reflect unmeasured confounding.” → retain. This is an interpretation with a material alternative explanation.

**Negative evidence.** “We found no statistically detectable difference (p = .18).” → retain. Do not rewrite as “The groups are equivalent” or “There is no difference.”

**Causal limit.** “This non-randomized design cannot identify a causal effect.” → retain if correct for this design. Do not turn it into “The design identifies the effect.” Nonrandomized studies can sometimes support causal estimates under explicit assumptions; do not infer the appropriate claim from a label alone.

**Vague magnitude.** “A number of participants improved somewhat.” → if the supplied result is 18/30 participants, “Eighteen of 30 participants improved.” Preserve the uncertainty about improvement magnitude until it is available. If neither quantity is available, keep a conservative qualitative statement and flag the missing evidence.

**Overstatement.** “These correlations conclusively prove the mechanism.” → “These correlations are consistent with the proposed mechanism.” Use even that interpretation only if supported; otherwise report the correlations themselves.

**Research gap.** “No studies have addressed this problem.” → “The studies reviewed here do not address [specific problem]” only if that is supported by the supplied review. “Few studies…” also requires evidence; it is not a safe automatic substitute.

**Reviewer response.** “We apologize that we were unable to fully address this very important point.” → “We have clarified [specific issue] in [verified section]. [Remaining constraint and consequence].” Do not claim changes, analyses, or section locations that have not been verified. Keep an apology where a real error warrants it.

**Translation.** Preserve epistemic stance and negation even if the target language prefers a different sentence structure. “可能与……有关” must not become an established causal relationship. Remove defensive framing only within the permitted translation/editing scope.
